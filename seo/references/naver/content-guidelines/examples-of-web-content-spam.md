# Examples of web content spam

[Source: Naver Search Advisor](https://searchadvisor.naver.com/guide/content-abusing)

Naver Search is continuously updating its spam detection algorithm to protect users and provide useful search results. As spam techniques have become more diverse due to recent technological advancements and the spread of AI-generated content, we will guide you through the main types below.
Sites and documents detected as spam may be subject to disadvantages such as exclusion from search results or lower rankings. Even if a site was operating normally, if related activity is confirmed, exposure may be limited after the confirmation point by comprehensively considering the impact on user experience.
If you believe that web content violates our spam policy, please notify us at [here](https://help.naver.com/inquiry/input.help?categoryNo=5749&serviceNo=5626&lang=ko). It may help you improve.

### Cloaking
This is a case where the content shown to search users and the content delivered to search engines are intentionally structured differently. This also applies to cases where completely different content is displayed only on a specific path or device based on the referrer or browser agent name, etc.

### Inappropriate redirect
This may apply if the user is forced to move to a document/site for purposes unrelated to the inquiry. In order to avoid detection, it also includes a method of moving to an unwanted page after a certain period of time or when going back.

### link spam
This is a case of attempting to expose a website by exploiting the security vulnerabilities of a normal operating site and inserting a large number of links into the page that lead to spam sites, or by creating a page consisting of only links. Neglected management paths or bulletin boards become prime targets, which can put the entire site at a disadvantage. In the case of bulletin boards where anyone can write, we recommend operating a membership system and maintaining a normal authentication process, or establishing a minimum defense device.

### Backlink Abuse
This is when you artificially create a large number of external links to increase the number of mentions of a specific keyword and then sell the site itself, or set up redirects and domain forwarding to transfer search qualities to other sites. Both the sold site and the forwarding target site may be penalized.

### Exploiting expired domains
This is a case where an expired domain previously used by a trustworthy institution or organization is purchased and then spam or harmful content is posted with the purpose of using the existing trust to increase search rankings. For example, this applies to posting unrelated content on domains used by facilities such as public institutions or schools.

### Macros and Manipulations
This is an act of manipulating the number of comments, visits, and recommendations by mobilizing multiple accounts for the purpose of influencing search rankings, or disguising it as popular content by artificially inflating traffic using an automated program.

### Hacked Content
This content has been installed without permission due to a security vulnerability on the site. This includes insertion of spam links, insertion of malicious scripts, and code that redirects users to harmful pages. It may also include cases where content unrelated to the original purpose is created or exposed by a third party due to insufficient domain or subdomain management settings, etc. Caution is required as this often occurs without the site owner being aware of it. Especially when spam content is designed to be exposed only to specific agents, it is difficult for general users to notice the damage.
To check if your site has been hacked, check the HTML collected by the actual crawler through Search Advisor [Webmaster Tools > Verification > URL Inspection]. If reasonable measures, such as security measures and content removal, have been quickly taken after recognizing the damage, you can request normalization of search exposure through [[[Report search exposure](https://help.naver.com/inquiry/input.help?categoryNo=5228&serviceNo=5626&lang=ko)].
Please refer to the Naver Security blog post “[Abandoned websites become hosts for phishing attacks](https://blog.naver.com/naversecurity/224003827355)” for help.

### Malicious code distribution
This occurs when installing or distributing harmful software such as malware or viruses without the user’s consent.

### hidden text
For the purpose of search exposure, it is a method of hiding ‘a large amount of keywords unrelated to the content of the text’ so that they are not visible to the user. This applies to cases where the font color is set to be the same as the background color, the font size is made extremely small, or the text is placed outside the screen.

### Keyword repetition/abuse
This refers to cases where the same keyword is intentionally repeated in the title or text for the sole purpose of appearing at the top of searches, or when a keyword with a high search volume is substituted for the actual name. It is characterized by the excessive appearance of certain words regardless of the natural flow of sentences.

### fishing castle
This is a case where search terms unrelated to the actual content are inserted into the title or body to only aim for search traffic. It is characterized by a discrepancy between the title and description and the actual content.

### Generate large amounts of low-quality content
This is a case of pointlessly publishing content in large quantities using the same or similar templates for the purpose of dominating search results. This method involves publishing dozens or more pages with only part of the sentence changed. This process may include the use of AI or automation programs, but it is not judged solely on the use of specific technologies.

### scraping
This occurs when content posted on another site is copied or mechanically modified through an automated program and posted almost verbatim. This also applies to cases where search engine results are taken directly from the search engine and are then clicked on as if they were own content.

### harmful content
Exposure of content that aims at, instigates, or aids crime, such as pornography or gambling, or sells or promotes illegal content or illegal products, may be restricted in order to comply with relevant laws or to protect users.

### Content that causes user confusion and harm
This includes content that is clearly false, such as incorrect phone numbers, addresses, application links, synthetic or created images or videos, or that may be mistaken for fact and cause confusion or substantial damage to users (for example, unauthorized collection of personal information through incorrect links).

### Similar public site
This is a site that steals government emblems, names, and contents to disguise itself as an official public institution and induces the collection of personal information or payment of fees.

### phishing
Phishing is a combination of the words ‘private data’ and ‘fishing’ and is a frequent fraud method. This also includes cases where, in order to steal Naver users' personal information, they are disguised as a Naver login page or a protection removal page and are tricked into entering the ID, password, and key personal information used by Naver. Please refer to the “[Beware of the spread of search engine-based phishing](https://blog.naver.com/naversecurity/223369092366)” notice on the Naver Security Blog.

### Impairment of service stability
This is an act of using Naver services in an abnormal way or interfering with the operation of the service. This includes mass registration of posts through automated programs, distribution and promotion of Naver information extraction programs, and distribution of malicious programs such as DDOS. For detailed information, please refer to [Green Internet](https://green.naver.com/content-account-home/CkE3eCUv).


In addition to the types introduced above, similar actions aimed at interfering with the normal judgment of the search system may also be considered spam. Additionally, even if it does not constitute spam, search exposure may be limited to protect users if inappropriate behavior that deceives users or causes substantial damage is identified.
Site operators who have objections to search exposure restrictions in accordance with this policy or who have completed corrective measures such as security measures may request normalization of search exposure.  Please submit the facts and details of corrective action along with the request through [here](https://help.naver.com/inquiry/input.help?categoryNo=5228&serviceNo=5626&lang=ko). Naver will review the submitted request within a reasonable period and take necessary action.
